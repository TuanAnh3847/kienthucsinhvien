Chương 1: Culture and Cross-Cultural Management

Mục tiêu trọng tâm: Hiểu bản chất/lớp/yếu tố quyết định của văn hóa; tác động đến kinh doanh; phản ứng xuyên văn hóa; EPRG và standardization–adaptation.
Learning block

1. What is culture?

Đặt nền bằng definitions, values/norms và quan hệ society–nation-state.

🧭

Culture / Văn hóa

Những cách suy nghĩ và hành vi chung được truyền qua gia đình/tổ chức xã hội, rồi được phát triển và củng cố bởi áp lực xã hội. Văn hóa là tri thức được tiếp thu để diễn giải trải nghiệm và tạo hành vi xã hội.
💎

Values / Giá trị

Các ý niệm trừu tượng về điều một nhóm cho là tốt, đúng và đáng mong muốn; tạo bối cảnh để các chuẩn mực được hình thành và biện minh.
📏

Norms / Chuẩn mực

Các quy tắc và hướng dẫn xã hội quy định hành vi phù hợp trong tình huống cụ thể. Folkways là quy ước thường nhật; mores là chuẩn mực cốt lõi đối với vận hành đời sống xã hội.
🏙️

Objective vs Subjective

Objective: công cụ, đường sá, chương trình TV, kiến trúc và hiện vật vật chất. Subjective: norms, values, ideas, customs, assumptions.
👥

Society

Một nhóm người chia sẻ một bộ giá trị và chuẩn mực chung.
🌐

Society ↔ Nation-state

Quan hệ không nhất thiết một-một: một quốc gia có thể chứa nhiều văn hóa và một văn hóa có thể trải qua nhiều quốc gia.

3. Determinants of culture

Concept map tương tác, sau đó đi sâu vào các yếu tố có nội dung rõ ràng.

Chọn một node

Nhấn vào từng yếu tố để xem nội dung chi tiết.

✝️

Christianity

Bài học mô tả đây là tôn giáo lớn nhất, hiện diện rộng ở châu Âu, châu Mỹ và các nước định cư bởi người châu Âu; Protestant work ethic được gắn với hard work, wealth creation và frugality.
☪️

Islam

Islam là một lối sống bao quát; kinh doanh được ủng hộ nhưng cách thực hành kinh doanh được quy định, với quan niệm con người là người quản lý tài sản được giao phó.
🕉️

Hinduism

Nhấn mạnh spiritual growth; vật chất/entrepreneurship có thể không được đặt trọng tâm tương tự một số tôn giáo khác và caste có thể ảnh hưởng mục tiêu thăng tiến.
☸️

Buddhism

Số liệu tham khảo ghi “about 350 millions followers”, nhấn mạnh spiritual growth/afterlife hơn thành tựu vật chất; không nhấn mạnh wealth creation và không ủng hộ caste system.
📚

Confucianism

Loyalty, reciprocal obligations và honesty là ba giáo huấn có thể làm giảm chi phí kinh doanh trong các xã hội ảnh hưởng Confucianism.
Social structure: individual/group & stratification

Group: association of two or more people with a shared identity who interact in structured ways based on common expectations. Western societies are described as focusing more on the individual while many Asian societies focus more on the group.

Social stratification: hierarchical categories/social strata. Societies differ in mobility between strata and the importance of strata in business contacts. A caste system is closed; a class system is open and position can change through achievement or luck.

Class consciousness: people perceive themselves by class background; where high, cross-class work relationships can be prescribed and strained.

Hệ thống chính trị và kinh tế — tóm tắt

Political system: the system of government in a nation, assessed in the bài học by collectivism ↔ individualism and democracy ↔ totalitarianism. The course defines democracy as government by the people, directly or through elected representatives; totalitarianism as one person/party exercising absolute control over all spheres of life and prohibiting opposition.

Economic systems: market economy, command economy, mixed economy. Các nhóm chính gồm: market = private ownership and supply/demand; command = government planning/state ownership; mixed = elements of both.

. Phần chính trị và kinh tế tập trung vào các nhóm nội dung đọc rõ.

Learning block

4. Why culture matters in business

Từ khác biệt văn hóa đến hệ quả quản trị và business risk.

Cultural difference→

Different frames of reference can change interpretation, behavior, negotiation and even handshake meaning.

Poor cross-cultural literacy→

Bài học warns ill-informed firms are unlikely to succeed; managers may hire local citizens or rotate executives abroad.

Culture→

Can influence technology transfer, managerial attitudes/ideology, business-government relations, rewards, risk, time horizon and formality.

Cross-cultural literacy

Hiểu cách khác biệt văn hóa giữa và trong các quốc gia có thể ảnh hưởng cách kinh doanh được thực hành.

Hand gestures

Course examples: “OK” can mean OK in U.S., money in Japan, “big zero” in France, insult in Brazil; thumbs-up is good in U.S. but offensive in parts of West Africa; V-sign orientation changes meaning in the U.K.

Handshake

U.S. firm; Asian generally gentle, with Korean exception noted as firm; British soft; French light and quick.

Culture change

Culture evolves over time; value-system change can be slow/painful, globalization brings cultural change, and economic progress is linked in the bài học with shifts toward individualism.

5. Responding to cultural difference

Phân biệt frame of reference và ba phản ứng với thực hành địa phương.

Face-to-face→

Meetings, communication, negotiation → language, knowledge/expertise, behavior, rituals.

Company-to-company→

Contracts, alliances, JVs, M&A → organization, hierarchy/decision making, labour relations, attitudes toward work.

Company-to-customer→

Marketing (4Ps), new product development → consumer preferences and “quality” of demand.

Objectives of cross-cultural management
  1. Increase company success in foreign markets by operating efficiently.
  2. Adapt corporate management processes to cultural differences in international markets.
  3. Prevent errors and conflicts in international cooperation.

Ethnocentrism

Đánh giá văn hóa khác từ vị trí văn hóa của chính mình; quản lý cần cảnh giác với niềm tin rằng văn hóa của mình vượt trội.

Cultural relativism

Đánh giá/hiểu hành vi từ khung tham chiếu của văn hóa đó.

Which response is this?

Resistance

Reject changing own/corporate behavior to local standards or expectations.

Acceptance

Accept a local aspect/procedure/observance without changing own behavior.

Adaptation

Change own/corporate behavior to local standards/expectations.

7. Standardize or adapt?

Đây không phải là công tắc nhị phân: global localization kết hợp cả hai.

Standardization ↔ Adaptation continuum

Globalization / StandardizationGlobal localizationAdaptation
Legal considerations

Product changes may respond to legal rules, often involving safety/health protection.

Cultural considerations

Preferences, language, customs and meanings can require modification.

Economic considerations

Personal incomes and infrastructure affect product demand.

Global localization

Mix standardization and customization to minimize costs while maximizing satisfaction — “think globally, act locally.”

Advice for managers in cross-cultural management
  • Deep understanding of cultural, economic, social and religious differences.
  • Recognize own ethnocentric perspective on management.
  • Interest in deepening cross-cultural understanding.
  • Recognize cultural shock and strategies to deal with it.
  • Detect/prevent/mediate cross-cultural conflict.
  • Remain open to new ideas, management processes, preferences and values.

3. Trompenaars dimension explorer

Bảy cặp — không thêm cặp thứ tám.

↔ 1

Universalism vs Particularism

Universalism: rules/standards applied broadly
Particularism: circumstances/relationships guide application
↔ 2

Individualism vs Communitarianism

Individualism: individual focus/responsibility
Communitarianism: group focus/joint responsibility
↔ 3

Neutral vs Affective / Emotional

Neutral: emotions controlled
Affective / Emotional: emotions expressed openly
↔ 4

Specific vs Diffuse

Specific: public/private spaces more separated
Diffuse: public/private spaces more linked
↔ 5

Achievement vs Ascription

Achievement: status from performance
Ascription: status from who/what a person is
↔ 6

Sequential time vs Synchronous time

Sequential time: one activity at a time; plans/appointments structured
Synchronous time: ⚠ Lưu ý: dòng này có cách diễn đạt dễ nhầm; hãy dựa vào dấu hiệu multi-task và relationship scheduling
↔ 7

Inner-directed vs Outer-directed

Inner-directed: people seek to control environment/outcomes
Outer-directed: people work with/adapt to environment and avoid conflict where possible

Pair matching — 7 dimensions

Dấu hiệu đàm phán theo Trompenaars
  • Universalist: work within rules, learn normal procedures, avoid expecting completed contracts to be renegotiated.
  • Particularist: be willing to adjust, stay flexible, keep knowledge current.
  • Individualist: one authorized negotiator may make quick decisions and commitments.
  • Communitarian: be patient and aim to build lasting relationships.
  • Neutral: put much on paper; lack of visible emotion does not mean lack of interest.
  • Emotional: do not be thrown off by animated expression; respond warmly.
  • Specific: get to the point, use agendas, avoid irrelevant status/title cues.
  • Diffuse: respect title/age/background connections; tolerate indirect/circuitous discussion.
  • Achievement: bring sufficient data/technical advisers/knowledge and respect counterpart expertise.
  • Ascription: include senior/formal position holders and respect counterpart status/influence.
  • Future-oriented: emphasize opportunities/scope and agree specific deadlines.
  • Past/present-oriented: emphasize history/tradition and be cautious about fixed deadlines.
  • Inner-directed: bài học suggests playing hardball/testing resilience while accepting wins/losses.
  • Outer-directed: be persistent/polite, maintain relationships, aim to win together.
Bài học-note on Time: ⚠ Lưu ý: dòng Synchronous Time có cách diễn đạt gần với Ascription. Hãy dựa vào ba dấu hiệu: multi-task, approximate appointments và schedules subordinate to relationships.

Motivation

A psychological process through which unsatisfied wants or needs lead to drives aimed at goals or incentives.
🎨

Intrinsic reward

Interest/enjoyment in task itself: enjoyment, purpose, growth, curiosity, passion, self-expression, fun.
🏆

Extrinsic reward

Outcome resulting from task: promotions, pay raises, bonuses, benefits, prizes, winning, perks.
🏢

Why firms care

Join; stay; reduce absenteeism/lateness; increase engagement; encourage organizational citizenship / extra effort.

Five-stage hierarchy + cultural qualification + Ví dụ.

Original assumptions
  • Lower-level needs must be satisfied before higher-level needs become motivators.
  • A satisfied need no longer motivates.
  • More ways exist to satisfy higher-level than lower-level needs.
International qualification
  • Course says needs may be similar, but cited study is dated.
  • MNCs should consider country/region profile; Maslow may be too Western.
  • Collectivist visual changes ordering/content and puts social belonging at the base.

4. Herzberg Two-Factor Theory

No dissatisfaction ≠ satisfaction.

Hygiene factors

Working conditions; coworker relations; policies/rules; supervisor quality; base wage/salary.

Absent → Dissatisfaction
Present → No dissatisfaction

Motivator factors

Achievement; recognition; responsibility; work itself; advancement; personal growth.

Absent → No satisfaction
Present → Satisfaction

Hygiene or Motivator?

High hygiene + High motivationBest Case Scenario
High hygiene + Low motivationComplacency
Low hygiene + High motivationUntapped Potential
Low hygiene + Low motivationPoor Results

6. Process theories

Equity, Goal Setting, Expectancy.

⚖️

Equity

Perceived fairness; inputs/outcomes/comparisons.
🎯

Goal Setting

Participation, difficulty, specificity, importance, feedback.
🔗

Expectancy

Effort → Performance → Outcomes/Rewards; E × I × V.
Inputs

time, effort, ability, loyalty, tolerance, flexibility, integrity, commitment, reliability, personal sacrifice…

⚖️
Outputs

pay, bonus, perks, benefits, security, recognition, development, reputation, praise, responsibility, enjoyment…

Perceived equitable treatment → positive job satisfaction. Perceived unfairness, especially relative to relevant others → dissatisfaction and attempts to restore equity. International support is mixed.

SpecificMeasurableAchievableRelevantTime-Bound

Course focus: participation in goal setting, goal difficulty, goal specificity, importance of objective, timely feedback.

International finding

Cited Norwegian employees preferred union-representative participation rather than individual participation in setting work goals; the U.S. participation result was not assumed to transfer directly.

Motivation = Expectancy × Instrumentality × Valence

Conceptual relationship only — no numerical scale or calculator is authorized.

Expectancy chain builder

Expectancy

Belief that effort will lead to performance. Maximize by ability selection, training, support, clear goals.

Instrumentality

Belief/confidence that performance leads to outcomes/rewards. Clarify contracts and performance–outcome links.

Valence

Value of possible rewards/outcomes to the individual. Identify needs and match rewards.

Theory → scenario matching

7. Applied motivation across cultures

Job design, work centrality and rewards.

Japan assembly line

Bài học: rapid pace for hours and little control over work activities.

Sweden assembly line

Bài học: more relaxed pace and a great deal of control over work activities.

U.S. assembly line

Bài học: typically between Japan and Sweden — less demanding pace than Japan, more structured than Sweden.

Job design = job content, methods used, and how the job relates to other jobs. Bài học says quality of work life may be directly related to country culture.

2. Five recurring international-business issue areas

Ethical dilemma

A situation in which none of the available alternatives seems ethically acceptable. Real-world decisions can be complex, hard to frame, and have consequences difficult to quantify; the right course is not always clear.

4. Philosophical approaches

Four straw men + four other bài học approaches.

Straw men

  • Friedman doctrine: only social responsibility is to increase profits while staying within the rules of law.
  • Cultural relativism: ethics culturally determined; firms adopt ethics of cultures where they operate.
  • Righteous moralist: home-country ethical standards should be followed abroad.
  • Naïve immoralist: if other firms ignore ethical norms in a host nation, a manager should not either.

Other approaches in the course

  • Utilitarian: consequences; best balance of good over bad.
  • Kantian: people as ends, never purely as means; dignity.
  • Rights: fundamental rights transcend national boundaries/cultures.
  • Justice: fair/equitable distribution; Rawls and veil of ignorance.

Philosophical-approach matching — 8 approaches

Learning block

5. Five-step ethical decision process

Exact bài học order must be preserved.

1 Identify stakeholders2 Determine violation3 Establish moral intent4 Ethical engagement5 Audit decisions

Drag-and-order the 5-step process

Desktop: kéo thả. Mobile/keyboard: dùng ↑ ↓ để đổi vị trí.

Step details
  1. Identify stakeholders: who a decision affects and how; internal stakeholders include employees/board/stockholders, external include customers/suppliers/unions.
  2. Determine violation: whether a proposed decision violates fundamental stakeholder rights.
  3. Establish moral intent: put moral concerns ahead when fundamental rights/key principles are violated.
  4. Ethical engagement: engage in ethical behavior.
  5. Audit decisions: review consistency with ethical principles; bài học says this is often overlooked yet critical.
